01 • OVERVIEW

Understanding the system

The Philippine criminal justice system is a network of institutions and processes that work together to prevent crime, investigate offenses, prosecute cases, adjudicate disputes, and administer sanctions and rehabilitation. It is an organized response of the State and society to criminal behavior. It involves different institutions, professionals, and community members who perform distinct roles in maintaining public safety and administering justice. The system also seeks to balance accountability with the protection of constitutional rights, ensuring that government authority is exercised within the limits of the law.

⌕

What is criminal justice?

It is the organized response of society to crime through law enforcement, prosecution, courts, corrections, and community participation. Criminal justice refers to the collection of laws, institutions, procedures, and practices used to prevent crime, investigate offenses, determine criminal responsibility, impose lawful penalties, and support rehabilitation. Its purpose is not simply to punish offenders. It also includes protecting victims, preserving public order, ensuring due process, and maintaining respect for human dignity.

Justice in action

Cases move through connected stages rather than existing as isolated events. From the reporting of an alleged offense to investigation, prosecution, court proceedings, judgment, and correctional processes, each stage can affect the next part of the case.

✓

Core idea

Due process, equal protection, presumption of innocence, and access to justice are central safeguards in criminal proceedings. These principles help prevent arbitrary government action and ensure that people involved in criminal proceedings are treated according to established legal standards.

02 • HISTORY

From colonial laws to a modern framework

The Philippine legal system developed through several historical periods. The timeline below highlights major milestones relevant to criminal law and justice institutions. Understanding this historical development helps students recognize how different legal traditions contributed to the present Philippine justice system.

1521–1898

Spanish period

Spanish colonial rule introduced and applied Spanish legal traditions. The Código Penal de 1870 influenced criminal law in the Philippines. During this period, Spanish legal concepts became part of the legal framework used in the colony and influenced the later development of Philippine criminal law.

1898–1902

Revolution & First Republic

The Philippine Revolution and the First Philippine Republic marked an important period of constitutional and governmental development. This period demonstrated the importance of establishing institutions of government, defining governmental powers, and creating legal structures for a developing nation.

1900s

American period

New institutions and laws were established. Act No. 3815, the Revised Penal Code, was enacted in 1930. The period also contributed to the development of courts, law enforcement institutions, procedural rules, and other government structures that influenced the modern justice system.

1973

1973 Constitution

A new constitutional framework reorganized government during a significant period in Philippine history. The Constitution established a framework for governmental powers and rights during an important stage of the country's political and legal development.

1987–Present

1987 Constitution & continuing reform

The 1987 Constitution strengthened the Bill of Rights and judicial safeguards. Criminal justice institutions continue to evolve through legislation, jurisprudence, technology, and reforms. The current framework continues to develop as new laws, court decisions, technologies, and institutional reforms respond to changing social and legal needs.

03 • EVOLUTION OF LAW

How Philippine criminal law evolves

Laws are not static. Congress enacts statutes, courts interpret them, agencies implement them, and later laws or decisions can modify how rules operate. The development of criminal law reflects changes in society, public safety needs, technology, human rights standards, and the changing nature of criminal activity.

04 • CRIMINAL & VICTIM

People at the center of justice

A criminal case involves more than a suspected offender. The justice process also recognizes victims, witnesses, investigators, prosecutors, judges, defense counsel, correctional personnel, and communities. Each participant has different rights, responsibilities, and roles. Understanding these roles helps students understand how the criminal justice process operates as a whole.

👤

Person accused

An accused person is entitled to constitutional and procedural safeguards, including the presumption of innocence and the right to counsel. The accused has the right to participate in proceedings according to law and to be treated fairly throughout the criminal process.

♡

Victim

Victims may experience physical, emotional, financial, and social harm. Victim assistance and protection are important components of a responsive justice system. Victims may also need access to information, support services, protection, and appropriate legal remedies provided by law.

◉

Witness

Witnesses provide information that may help establish facts. Their safety and participation can be crucial to case development. Witness testimony and other evidence may help courts determine the facts relevant to a case, subject to the rules governing admissibility and evaluation of evidence.

⚖

Justice actors

Law enforcement officers, prosecutors, courts, and corrections personnel each have distinct responsibilities governed by law. Their cooperation is important because criminal justice requires coordination between investigation, prosecution, adjudication, correction, and community participation.

05 • JUSTICE

What does justice require?

Justice in criminal proceedings is connected with lawful procedure, reliable fact-finding, protection of rights, accountability, and fair treatment of people involved in the process. It requires authorities to exercise their powers within constitutional and legal limits while providing appropriate procedures for determining criminal responsibility.

⚖

Rule of law

No person is above the law. Government power must be exercised within constitutional and legal limits. The rule of law means that governmental authorities, institutions, and individuals are expected to act according to established legal rules and procedures.

Due process Fair procedures before deprivation of life, liberty, or property. It helps ensure that legal decisions are made through established and lawful procedures.
Equal protection People are entitled to equal protection of the laws. This principle forms part of the constitutional protection against arbitrary treatment.
Accountability Public authority and criminal conduct are subject to legal accountability. Individuals and institutions must answer for actions that violate applicable laws.
Rehabilitation Correctional approaches can include reform and reintegration. Rehabilitation seeks to help persons under correctional supervision prepare for lawful participation in society.
06 • THE FIVE PILLARS

The architecture of criminal justice

A commonly taught Philippine criminal justice framework identifies five interconnected pillars. No single pillar operates as the entire system. The five pillars are law enforcement, prosecution, courts, corrections, and the community. Each pillar performs a different function while contributing to the broader administration of criminal justice.

Police and other authorized agencies prevent and investigate crime, preserve evidence, and make lawful arrests. Law enforcement also performs public safety functions and assists in maintaining peace and order while operating within constitutional and statutory limitations.

07 • CONSTITUTION

Constitutional foundations

The 1987 Constitution provides the fundamental framework for government and includes the Bill of Rights, which protects people against unlawful government action. Constitutional rights are particularly important in criminal justice because government authorities have powers that can directly affect a person's liberty, privacy, property, and other rights.

🇵🇭
1987
CONSTITUTION
01
Due process

No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. Due process provides protection against arbitrary government action and requires lawful procedures before certain rights may be affected.

02
Searches & seizures

Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures is a constitutional safeguard. The law establishes requirements governing searches and seizures in order to protect individuals from unreasonable government intrusion.

03
Rights of the accused

Article III, Section 14 contains important rights of an accused in criminal prosecutions. These protections are designed to ensure that an accused person receives a fair opportunity to defend against criminal allegations.

04
Presumption of innocence

Every person is presumed innocent until the contrary is proved. This principle places the burden of proving criminal responsibility according to the applicable rules of law rather than requiring an accused person to prove innocence.

10 • ARREST

Arrest: a lawful deprivation of liberty

An arrest is the taking of a person into custody so that the person may answer for an offense. Philippine procedure distinguishes arrests made with a warrant from recognized warrantless arrests. Because an arrest affects personal liberty, officers must comply with constitutional safeguards and the requirements of applicable procedural rules.

⛓
RULE 113
RULES OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE
01

With warrant

A warrant of arrest may be issued by a judge upon the constitutionally and procedurally required showing of probable cause. The judicial process for issuing a warrant serves as an important safeguard concerning the lawful exercise of government authority.

02

Warrantless arrest

Rule 113, Section 5 recognizes specific situations in which an arrest may be made without a warrant, subject to the rule's requirements. Warrantless arrests are not automatically lawful simply because a person is suspected of committing an offense. The circumstances must fall within a legally recognized exception.

03

Rights matter

Arrest and custodial investigation implicate constitutional and statutory safeguards. Officers and authorities must follow lawful procedures. Persons in custody may have important constitutional rights, including protections concerning counsel and custodial investigation as provided by law.

11 • LAW ENFORCEMENT

Protecting the public while respecting rights

Law enforcement is responsible for public safety, crime prevention, investigation, and lawful enforcement. Its powers are bounded by the Constitution and statutes. Law enforcement personnel may perform different functions depending on their agency and legal authority. Their duties include responding to incidents, gathering information, protecting people, investigating offenses, and enforcing applicable laws.

PNP

Philippine National Police

Primary national police organization responsible for law enforcement and public safety functions. Its personnel perform duties involving crime prevention, investigation, public safety, maintenance of peace and order, and other responsibilities established by law.

NBI

National Bureau of Investigation

Investigates certain serious and complex crimes and performs other functions assigned by law. The agency may assist in specialized criminal investigations and other activities within its statutory authority.

PDEA

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency

Lead agency for enforcement of laws against dangerous drugs under its governing statutes. Its responsibilities include enforcement, investigation, and coordination related to violations of applicable dangerous drugs laws.

12 • COURTS

The judiciary and adjudication

Courts resolve cases according to the Constitution, statutes, procedural rules, and jurisprudence. Different courts have different jurisdiction and functions. Judicial proceedings provide a formal process for determining legal issues and resolving disputes. In criminal cases, courts consider the evidence and arguments presented according to applicable rules and legal standards.

01
Supreme Court Highest court; exercises powers provided by the Constitution. It exercises constitutional and judicial powers assigned to it by the Constitution and laws.
02
Court of Appeals Reviews decisions within its appellate jurisdiction. It performs appellate functions in accordance with the Constitution, statutes, and applicable procedural rules.
03
Regional Trial Courts Exercise original and appellate jurisdiction as provided by law. These courts handle cases within the jurisdiction assigned to them by law.
04
First-level courts Metropolitan, Municipal, Municipal Circuit, and Municipal Trial Courts. These courts exercise jurisdiction over matters assigned to them by applicable Philippine laws.
13 • CORRECTIONS

Custody, rehabilitation & reintegration

Corrections includes institutional and community-based approaches to carrying out lawful sentences and supporting rehabilitation. The correctional component may involve custody, security, rehabilitation programs, education, skills development, and preparation for reintegration into the community.

BEYOND PUNISHMENT

Rehabilitation is part of the justice conversation.

Correctional systems face the continuing challenge of balancing custody, security, humane treatment, rehabilitation, and preparation for return to the community. Rehabilitation may include programs that develop education, skills, behavior, and social support so that persons under correctional supervision can prepare for lawful reintegration.

14 • CHALLENGES

Issues that shape justice delivery

Criminal justice systems face operational and social challenges. Understanding them helps students evaluate reforms and the experience of people within the system. These issues can affect how quickly cases are resolved, how accessible justice is to the public, how institutions operate, and how communities experience the justice system.

01

Case delays

Backlogs and procedural delays can affect the timely resolution of cases. Delays may result from different factors, including the volume of cases, procedural requirements, availability of resources, and other circumstances affecting judicial proceedings.

02

Access to justice

Distance, cost, legal assistance, and information can affect people's ability to participate effectively. Access to justice involves the ability of people to understand their rights, obtain appropriate assistance, and use available legal mechanisms.

03

Detention conditions

Overcrowding and resource constraints can create serious correctional management challenges. Proper detention conditions are important for security, humane treatment, health, safety, and the administration of correctional facilities.

04

Technology & evidence

Digital evidence creates opportunities and new demands for lawful collection, preservation, and presentation. Modern criminal investigations may involve electronic records, digital communications, surveillance systems, and other forms of technology-related evidence.

05

Public trust

Transparency, accountability, professionalism, and lawful conduct influence confidence in justice institutions. Public trust can be affected by how institutions perform their responsibilities and how effectively they protect rights and enforce the law.

06

Reintegration

Successful reentry can involve employment, family support, community acceptance, and access to services. Reintegration is important because people returning to society may need opportunities and support to establish stable and lawful lives.
