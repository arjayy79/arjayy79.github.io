01 • OVERVIEW

Understanding the system

The Philippine criminal justice system is a network of institutions and processes that work together to prevent crime, investigate offenses, prosecute cases, adjudicate disputes, and administer sanctions and rehabilitation.

⌕

What is criminal justice?

It is the organized response of society to crime through law enforcement, prosecution, courts, corrections, and community participation.

LAW • RIGHTS • PROCESS • ACCOUNTABILITY
COURT

Justice in action

Cases move through connected stages rather than existing as isolated events.

✓

Core idea

Due process, equal protection, presumption of innocence, and access to justice are central safeguards in criminal proceedings.

02 • HISTORY

From colonial laws to a modern framework

The Philippine legal system developed through several historical periods. The timeline below highlights major milestones relevant to criminal law and justice institutions.

1521–1898

Spanish period

Spanish colonial rule introduced and applied Spanish legal traditions. The Código Penal de 1870 influenced criminal law in the Philippines.

1898–1902

Revolution & First Republic

The Philippine Revolution and the First Philippine Republic marked an important period of constitutional and governmental development.

1900s

American period

New institutions and laws were established. Act No. 3815, the Revised Penal Code, was enacted in 1930.

1973

1973 Constitution

A new constitutional framework reorganized government during a significant period in Philippine history.

1987–Present

1987 Constitution & continuing reform

The 1987 Constitution strengthened the Bill of Rights and judicial safeguards. Criminal justice institutions continue to evolve through legislation, jurisprudence, technology, and reforms.

How Philippine criminal law evolves

Laws are not static. Congress enacts statutes, courts interpret them, agencies implement them, and later laws or decisions can modify how rules operate.

04 • CRIMINAL & VICTIM

People at the center of justice

A criminal case involves more than a suspected offender. The justice process also recognizes victims, witnesses, investigators, prosecutors, judges, defense counsel, correctional personnel, and communities.

👤

Person accused

An accused person is entitled to constitutional and procedural safeguards, including the presumption of innocence and the right to counsel.

♡

Victim

Victims may experience physical, emotional, financial, and social harm. Victim assistance and protection are important components of a responsive justice system.

◉

Witness

Witnesses provide information that may help establish facts. Their safety and participation can be crucial to case development.

⚖

Justice actors

Law enforcement officers, prosecutors, courts, and corrections personnel each have distinct responsibilities governed by law.

05 • JUSTICE

What does justice require?

Justice in criminal proceedings is connected with lawful procedure, reliable fact-finding, protection of rights, accountability, and fair treatment of people involved in the process.

⚖

Rule of law

No person is above the law. Government power must be exercised within constitutional and legal limits.

Due process Fair procedures before deprivation of life, liberty, or property.
Equal protection People are entitled to equal protection of the laws.
Accountability Public authority and criminal conduct are subject to legal accountability.
Rehabilitation Correctional approaches can include reform and reintegration.

10 • ARREST

Arrest: a lawful deprivation of liberty

An arrest is the taking of a person into custody so that the person may answer for an offense. Philippine procedure distinguishes arrests made with a warrant from recognized warrantless arrests.

⛓
RULE 113
RULES OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE
01

With warrant

A warrant of arrest may be issued by a judge upon the constitutionally and procedurally required showing of probable cause.

02

Warrantless arrest

Rule 113, Section 5 recognizes specific situations in which an arrest may be made without a warrant, subject to the rule's requirements.

03

Rights matter

Arrest and custodial investigation implicate constitutional and statutory safeguards. Officers and authorities must follow lawful procedures.

11 • LAW ENFORCEMENT

Protecting the public while respecting rights

Law enforcement is responsible for public safety, crime prevention, investigation, and lawful enforcement. Its powers are bounded by the Constitution and statutes.

PNP

Philippine National Police

Primary national police organization responsible for law enforcement and public safety functions.

NBI

National Bureau of Investigation

Investigates certain serious and complex crimes and performs other functions assigned by law.

PDEA

Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency

Lead agency for enforcement of laws against dangerous drugs under its governing statutes.

12 • COURTS

The judiciary and adjudication

Courts resolve cases according to the Constitution, statutes, procedural rules, and jurisprudence. Different courts have different jurisdiction and functions.

01
Supreme Court Highest court; exercises powers provided by the Constitution.
02
Court of Appeals Reviews decisions within its appellate jurisdiction.
03
Regional Trial Courts Exercise original and appellate jurisdiction as provided by law.
04
First-level courts Metropolitan, Municipal, Municipal Circuit, and Municipal Trial Courts.
13 • CORRECTIONS

Custody, rehabilitation & reintegration

Corrections includes institutional and community-based approaches to carrying out lawful sentences and supporting rehabilitation.

BEYOND PUNISHMENT

Rehabilitation is part of the justice conversation.

Correctional systems face the continuing challenge of balancing custody, security, humane treatment, rehabilitation, and preparation for return to the community.

14 • CHALLENGES

Issues that shape justice delivery

Criminal justice systems face operational and social challenges. Understanding them helps students evaluate reforms and the experience of people within the system.

01

Case delays

Backlogs and procedural delays can affect the timely resolution of cases.

02

Access to justice

Distance, cost, legal assistance, and information can affect people's ability to participate effectively.

03

Detention conditions

Overcrowding and resource constraints can create serious correctional management challenges.

04

Technology & evidence

Digital evidence creates opportunities and new demands for lawful collection, preservation, and presentation.

05

Public trust

Transparency, accountability, professionalism, and lawful conduct influence confidence in justice institutions.

06

Reintegration

Successful reentry can involve employment, family support, community acceptance, and access to services.
